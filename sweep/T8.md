### A.1 (a) Company graphs built from text with LLMs

### huang2026cross — Cross-Stock Predictability via LLM-Augmented Semantic Networks
- Citation: Huang, Y., Fan, Z., Hu, K., Ye, Y., *Cross-Stock Predictability via LLM-Augmented Semantic Networks*, arXiv:2604.19476, 2026.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19476 (FT: https://arxiv.org/html/2604.19476)
- First public: v1 21 Apr 2026 (v2 26 Apr 2026); Venue: preprint (q-fin.PM/GN/ST); Peer-reviewed: no
- Verification: [FT]
- Summary: The candidate graph is built from the top-K (K=5) cosine neighbours of 10-K risk-factor embeddings. DeepSeek-Chat (temperature 0) then classifies each candidate pair from 10-K Item 1, product/segment text and competitor sentences, with firms anonymised as "Firm A"/"Firm B". The taxonomy is competitor / supply_chain / complementary / substitute / peer / unrelated. Competitor edges are removed and substitute edges down-weighted, and the remaining edges feed a pairs-style mean-reversion signal. On S&P 500 constituents (CRSP, 2011–2019 backtest), long-short Sharpe rises from 0.742 to 0.820. Robustness checks cover a random graph (Sharpe 0.541, not significant), a 2-digit SIC graph (0.792) and K ∈ {3, 5, 10}.
- Graph type: static per year (rolling); homogeneous nodes; typed (multi-relational) undirected edges; unsigned in form, though relation types are effectively signed by their weighting.
- Task: ranking / cross-sectional return prediction (trading signal)
- Data: CRSP (licensed) + SEC 10-K (public). Partly public.
- Code available? not stated
- What it leaves open: Only one LLM and one prompt are tested, with no prompt, model or threshold variants. The LLM's knowledge cutoff relative to the 2011–2019 backtest is not stated, and memorisation is not tested; only anonymisation is used. The LLM graph (0.820) beats SIC (0.792) by only a small margin, and no significance test of that difference is reported.

### yang2026llm — LLM Latent Edge Measurement: Point-in-Time Economic Graphs for Quantitative Investing from Corporate Disclosures
- Citation: Yang, F., Zhang, L., *LLM Latent Edge Measurement: Point-in-Time Economic Graphs for Quantitative Investing from Corporate Disclosures*, arXiv:2607.15640, 2026.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15640 (FT: https://arxiv.org/html/2607.15640)
- First public: v1 17 Jul 2026; Venue: preprint (stat.AP); Peer-reviewed: no
- Verification: [FT]
- Summary: Treats building a firm adjacency matrix as a *measurement* problem. Seven filing agents and two "knowledge" agents (parametric memory only, one of them prompted to be sceptical) produce evidence records with verbatim quotes. Records are fused by weighted logit voting around a sparse prior. A shared-counterparty layer is weighted by IDF, and edges are kept by relative per-firm quantile thresholding (τ = 0.6, floor 0.04). On 42 Nasdaq-100 firms this gives 149 directed edges. An adversarial web-search audit of 30 edges finds 88% confirmed for weight ≥ 0.1, and all refuted edges are low-weight. Edges fall within-sector 1.9× more often than random. Ablations show IDF filtering and relative thresholding matter.
- Graph type: static snapshot (point-in-time by filing date); directed, weighted; two layers (direct + shared-counterparty)
- Task: other (graph measurement / construction). The authors explicitly make no return claims.
- Data: SEC EDGAR 10-K/10-Q/20-F (public). Public.
- Code available? Stated as "released with the paper" (≈950 evidence records + matrices); no URL in the text.
- What it leaves open: One snapshot of 42 firms, one unnamed "Claude" model, and a 30-edge audit. 28.3% of mentions are category-level ("uranium suppliers") and cannot be resolved. The authors say the knowledge channel cannot be used for historical backtests because of look-ahead bias. There is no dynamic version and no downstream test.

### kose2026reconstructing — Reconstructing temporal multi-relational firm networks at scale using large language models. The case of the semiconductor industry
- Citation: Köse, S., Diem, C., Dervic, E., et al. (incl. Klimek, P.), *Reconstructing temporal multi-relational firm networks at scale using large language models*, arXiv:2605.15842, 2026.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.15842 (FT: https://arxiv.org/html/2605.15842v1)
- First public: v1 15 May 2026; Venue: preprint (also listed as an INET Oxford working paper 2026-14 in search snippets); Peer-reviewed: no
- Verification: [FT]
- Summary: Uses Common Crawl snapshots (two per year, 2015–2025) of firm websites for ~21k ORBIS semiconductor firms. GPT-4o-mini (≈130 USD) assigns each domain pair one of: supplies, supplied-by, partners, owns, owned-by, none. Precision is 0.884 and F1 0.784 on 100 manually annotated pages. About 22% of overlapping edges are in S&P Capital IQ, which is 2.3× the configuration-model expectation (z = 23). The network shows a 9% edge drop during the 2022 chip shortage, rising NVIDIA centrality, and a 2024–25 contraction of US–China links that the authors attribute to export controls ("strategic decoupling").
- Graph type: temporal (yearly snapshots); multi-relational, directed; heterogeneous by country
- Task: other (network reconstruction + descriptive temporal network analysis)
- Data: Common Crawl, OECD ICIO and BACI are public. ORBIS and S&P Capital IQ (validation) are licensed. Partly public.
- Code available? on request only
- What it leaves open: No stock returns are used. Edge timestamps are only crawl-snapshot years. The authors do not say whether the decoupling they observe is priced, or whether it can be predicted (link prediction / anomaly detection). The method is not applied beyond semiconductors.

### niu2026relational — Relational Probing: LM-to-Graph Adaptation for Financial Prediction
- Citation: Niu, Y., Jin, C., Dolphin, R., Dong, R., *Relational Probing: LM-to-Graph Adaptation for Financial Prediction*, arXiv:2604.10212, 2026.
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.10212 (FT: https://arxiv.org/html/2604.10212)
- First public: v1 11 Apr 2026; Venue: preprint (cs.CL); Peer-reviewed: no
- Verification: [FT]
- Summary: Replaces an LM's output head with a "relation head", a bilinear scorer over ticker embeddings attending to hidden states. Small Qwen3 models (0.6B/1.7B/4B) produce daily stock graphs from news (adjacency threshold τ = 0.5). These graphs are trained end-to-end with an LSTM+GAT three-class movement classifier. The method beats a news co-occurrence graph, but absolute gains are small (best MCC 0.056, AUC 0.557).
- Graph type: dynamic (daily); homogeneous; weighted
- Task: classification (3-class movement)
- Data: news dataset from Niu et al. (2024) with ticker labels. Public status not stated.
- Code available? not stated
- What it leaves open: The induced graphs are never inspected for economic meaning. Labels use a full-sample standard deviation (a possible leakage source). Only one model family and one dataset are used, and no variance is reported. No industry/GICS baseline is used in experiments.

### zhou2025zero — Zero-Shot Extraction of Stock Relationship Graphs with LLMs
- Citation: Zhou, H., Costa Sperb, L. F., Ma, T., *Zero-Shot Extraction of Stock Relationship Graphs with LLMs*, Proc. FinNLP Workshop @ EMNLP 2025.
- Link: https://preview.aclanthology.org/ingest-emnlp/2025.finnlp-2.2.pdf
- First public: 2025 (EMNLP 2025 workshop proceedings); Venue: FinNLP @ EMNLP 2025; Peer-reviewed: yes (workshop)
- Verification: [FT]
- Summary: GPT-4o-mini is prompted for *every* S&P 500 pair, using only its prior knowledge with no text input. It assigns one of 24 S&P Global Business Relationship types plus a confidence score. Rare types are pruned, leaving 13. The resulting graph feeds RGCN/RGAT ranking models. The test period (Oct 2023 – Dec 2024) starts after the model's stated knowledge cutoff. The LLM graph beats Wikidata and GICS graphs (e.g. RGCN top-1 Sharpe 1.176 vs 1.011 for GICS).
- Graph type: static; multi-relational, directed
- Task: ranking (stock return ranking)
- Data: S&P 500 prices (public via standard sources); 452 tickers "with full trading records". Public.
- Code available? not stated
- What it leaves open: The universe is survivor-filtered (only tickers with full trading records). The graph is built from parametric knowledge at the time of querying, so edges may encode post-2023 information even though returns are out of sample. Only one LLM is used, and confidence thresholds are not varied.

### chen2026sttgexplainer — ST-TGExplainer: Disentangling Stability and Transition Patterns for Temporal GNN Interpretability
- Citation: Chen, H., Zheng, X., Jiao, P., et al. (incl. Pan, S.), *ST-TGExplainer*, arXiv:2605.19822; PMLR 306 (ICML 2026).
- Link: https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19822 (FT: https://arxiv.org/html/2605.19822); PMLR page seen: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/chen26gx.html (not opened)
- First public: v1 19 May 2026; Venue: ICML 2026 (PMLR 306 header in the HTML); Peer-reviewed: yes (per header)
- Verification: [FT]
- Summary: A self-explaining temporal GNN that separates *stability* patterns (repeated past interactions) from *transition* patterns (first-time interactions), trained with a disentangled information-bottleneck objective. Evaluated with ACC-AUC and CoDy-style necessity/sufficiency metrics.
- Graph type: temporal (continuous-time event graph); homogeneous
- Task: link prediction + explanation
- Data: Wikipedia, Reddit, UCI, Enron, USLegis, Can.Parl. **No financial data.**
- Code available? https://github.com/hjchen-hdu/ST-TGExplainer

### sussek2026explaining — Explaining Temporal Graph Predictions With Shapley Values

### barbiero2026trade — Trade Wars on the Income Statement: The Effects of Tariffs on Firms

### mukhia2026structural — Structural Dynamics of G5 Stock Markets During Exogenous Shocks: A Random Matrix Theory-Based Complexity Gap Approach

## C. Crowding estimate
- Approx. number of 2025–2026 papers on this theme that I saw (opened + seen): ~75 across the seven sub-themes (49 opened as 2025–26, plus ~25 seen).
- By sub-theme (my judgement from what surfaced):
  - (a) LLM/text-built company graphs: **moderate and growing fast**. About 12 papers in 2025–26, almost all preprints, from many small groups. No shared benchmark, and no study varies construction choices systematically.
  - (b) Temporal-GNN explainers: **active in core ML** (ICLR 2026, ICML 2026, several preprints) but **thin in finance**. None of the opened explainers uses a financial or company graph. Finance "interpretable" models (TRACE, CSHT) are not tested for faithfulness.
  - (c) Geopolitical/tariff shocks via networks: **econ-heavy, ML-thin**. Fed Boston, IMF and RIETI papers exist, but use proprietary networks (FactSet, transaction data) and one-hop exposure. RMT/correlation work covers the 2025 tariff at market level only.
  - (d) Evaluation rigour: **growing for LLMs** (Kong 2026, FINSABER at KDD 2026, LAP test, Look-Ahead-Bench), **essentially absent for stock graphs/GNNs**.
  - (e) Hypergraph stock prediction: **crowded at the architecture level**. Many 2025 model papers, mostly Chinese groups, on NASDAQ/NYSE/CSI/S&P benchmarks. Interpretive or validation work on hyperedges is thin.
  - (f) "Is the learned graph meaningful?": **thin**. Only a 2024 background paper (Niu et al.) and indirect evidence: random-graph and SIC baselines in huang2026cross, and the metric-dependence in wade2026do.
  - (g) European markets: **very thin**. No AEX/Euronext/STOXX 600 graph paper was found in 2025–26. The only European items are a UK FTSE100 GNN preprint and G5 RMT (market level). Cross-market graph work covers US→China and US→Asia (CrossAlpha) but skips Europe.
- Dominant venues / groups / countries: arXiv q-fin/cs.LG preprints; ICAIF, FinNLP@EMNLP, ACL; ICML/ICLR for explainers; KDD D&B for evaluation. Groups seen repeatedly: UCD Dublin (Niu, Dong: 3 related papers), Edinburgh/Oxford (Ma, Cucuringu: zero-shot graphs, FINSABER, US–China bipartite), CSH Vienna (Klimek), Boston Fed, plus many Chinese groups (ECNU/Aalborg, Tongji, HKUST-GZ) for hypergraph and KG models.
- Shared benchmarks and datasets: S&P 500 (dominant); NASDAQ/NYSE 2013–2017 (Feng et al. 2019), which is still used in 2026 (GAPNet, Hermes); CSI 300; SPNews; SEC 10-K text; Wikipedia/Reddit/MOOC/UCI/Enron for explainers.
- Typical evaluation weaknesses observed:
  - **Survivor-only universes**: zhou2025zero keeps "452 tickers with full trading records"; the NASDAQ/NYSE 2013–2017 benchmark; FTSE100 79 firms. Kong 2026 finds only 1.2% of LLM-finance papers mention survivorship.
  - **LLM knowledge leakage into graph edges**: parametric-knowledge graphs in zhou2025zero; DeepSeek cutoff not stated in huang2026cross; yang2026llm explicitly warns about this.
  - **Full-sample normalisation in labels**: niu2026relational.
  - **Single LLM / single prompt, no construction-variance analysis**: huang2026cross, zhou2025zero, yang2026llm.
  - **Tiny audits**: yang2026llm, n = 30 edges.
  - **No variance across seeds**: niu2026relational.
  - **Explanation claims without faithfulness tests**: ding2026trace, harit2025from.
  - **Simulation-only evidence**: zhang2026llm.
  - **Explainers never evaluated on financial graphs.**
- Verdict: **moderate overall, but thin at the intersections.** Each sub-theme on its own has 2025–2026 activity (LLM graphs and hypergraph models especially). The combinations relevant to this thesis are nearly empty: (i) construction-choice sensitivity and look-ahead contamination of LLM-built company graphs; (ii) temporal-GNN explainers on company graphs; (iii) public-data, multi-hop network propagation of the 2025 tariff shock; (iv) meaning-audits of learned stock graphs or hypergraphs; (v) anything on Euronext/STOXX 600. These are niches where creativity, rather than a new architecture, is the contribution, which matches the supervisor's advice.

## D. Open gaps and niche ideas (thesis-sized, public data)

**D1. "Graph-construction multiverse" for LLM-built company graphs (specification-curve analysis).**
Gap: huang2026cross, zhou2025zero and yang2026llm each use one LLM and one prompt, with at most K or threshold sweeps. fukada2026snippet shows that the source choice (snippets vs full text) changes recall by ~20×. wade2026do shows that the "best" graph depends on the metric.
Thesis: from SEC 10-K text (public), build ~20–50 company graphs over a grid of choices:
- LLM family/size (open-weight Qwen/Llama vs one API model);
- evidence-grounded vs parametric-only prompting;
- anonymised vs named firms;
- entity-resolution strictness;
- top-K vs relative-quantile thresholds (yang2026llm);
- relation taxonomy granularity.
Report (i) graph-to-graph agreement (edge Jaccard, spectral distance, community NMI), (ii) agreement with a small hand-labelled audit set, and (iii) the *spread* of downstream results on a fixed simple model (network momentum / RGCN ranking). The main output is a "robustness curve" showing how much of the reported LLM-graph gain survives construction choices. The design is cheap, reproducible and in Python, and supervisor-fit is high (text → knowledge graph).

**D2. Edge-level look-ahead test: do LLM-generated stock graphs leak the future?**
Gap: gao2025detecting's LAP test and benhenda2026look address *forecasts*, not *relations*. zhou2025zero's graph comes purely from GPT-4o-mini memory. yang2026llm states that knowledge-channel edges cannot be point-in-time. kong2026evaluating gives a temporal-sanitation checklist but ignores graphs.
Thesis:
1. Define an edge-level lookahead propensity: ask an LLM whether firm A and firm B were related "as of date t" for relations that provably started after t. Ground truth for start dates comes from EDGAR filing dates of 10-K customer/supplier mentions, or Common Crawl snapshots as in kose2026reconstructing.
2. Estimate the contamination rate per model and cutoff.
3. Re-run a zhou2025zero-style GNN with a filing-only point-in-time graph versus a parametric graph, before and after the model's cutoff.

The thesis delivers a reusable diagnostic, with a clear yes/no question and public data.

**D3. Temporal-GNN explainers on company event graphs, with explanations validated against filings and used as a leakage auditor.**
Gap: chen2026sttgexplainer (ICML 2026), sussek2026explaining and lu2026training (ICLR 2026) all evaluate on Wikipedia, Reddit, MOOC or Enron, with no financial graphs. ding2026trace and harit2025from claim interpretability without faithfulness tests. sussek2026explaining found a TGAT timestamp leak *through* an explainer.
Thesis:
1. Build a continuous-time company event graph (LLM-extracted news/filing events + co-movement events) and train TGN/TGAT/GraphMixer for temporal link prediction ("will A and B co-move or co-appear?").
2. Apply TGNNSHAP / ST-TGExplainer / TemGX.
3. Measure faithfulness (fidelity/AUFSC) and *economic plausibility*: the share of top-explanatory past events that match a documented 10-K relation.
4. Test whether ST-TGExplainer's "transition" events coincide with new contracts or acquisitions.

Twist: inject known leakage (future-dated events, full-sample normalisation as in niu2026relational) and test whether explainers flag it. Strong fit with Frank's XAI background.

**D4. Public-data, multi-hop propagation of the 2025 US tariff shock through an LLM-extracted supplier/customer graph, with Europe included.**
Gap: barbiero2026trade uses one-hop FactSet propagation (proprietary). beyer2026trade explains only ~20% of the 2 April decline, with no network. mukhia2026structural covers the 2025 tariff at RMT market level only. saizarurtueta2026cross (MSc thesis) runs an event study with no network and names GVC centrality as future work. bonnet2026throwing shows that competitors can *benefit* (signed spillovers).
Thesis:
- Build a supplier/customer/competitor graph from 10-K text (S&P 500) and, for Europe, from annual reports (e.g. STOXX 600 / AEX firms; see E for unverified ESEF access).
- Compute direct, 1-hop and 2-hop *signed* tariff exposure: suppliers and customers negative, substitutes or competitors of exposed firms possibly positive.
- Test whether network exposure explains abnormal returns on the 13 announcement dates and the July 2025 US–EU deal beyond direct exposure, the GICS graph and the correlation graph.

The episode is clean, recent, public and very much a 2026 topic.

**D5. "Do learned stock graphs mean anything?": a meaning-audit of structure-learning and hypergraph models.**
Gap: niu2026gapnet, tan2025magnet, park2025dynamic, qiu2025hermes and luo2025ftscommdetector learn graphs or hyperedges but do not compare them with real relations. niu2024evaluating [BACKGROUND] proposes interpretation-first evaluation that the same group's 2026 models do not apply. huang2026cross shows that a random graph of matched density is not significant, while SIC and LLM graphs are close to each other.
Thesis:
1. Take 3–4 models with public code (MaGNet, park2025dynamic, H3M-SSMoEs; GAPNet if code appears) and retrain on a *point-in-time, survivor-inclusive* universe.
2. Extract the learned adjacency/incidence over time.
3. Measure alignment with GICS, 10-K text relations (D1 graph), correlation and degree-preserving random rewiring.
4. Run "graph necessity" ablations: replace the learned graph with a randomised graph of the same density and test whether performance survives.

Positive and negative results are both publishable. This is evaluation-rigour work the supervisor would value.

**D6. Higher-order relations from text: are LLM-extracted hyperedges more than their clique projection?**
Gap: hypergraph stock models build hyperedges from industry codes or correlation (park2025dynamic, qiu2025hermes) or learn them opaquely (tan2025magnet). yang2026llm's shared-counterparty layer is effectively a hyperedge ("firms sharing supplier k") flattened to pairs with IDF weights. kose2026reconstructing extracts partnerships and joint ventures that are naturally n-ary.
Thesis: extract n-ary relations from filings and news (consortia, shared suppliers, joint ventures, co-named competitor sets) as hyperedges. Compare a hypergraph model against (i) its clique projection, (ii) industry hyperedges and (iii) learned hyperedges for co-movement or crash-contagion prediction. This answers a sharp scientific question about higher-order structure.

**D7. Europe as an out-of-distribution test: US→Euronext/STOXX 600 cross-market transfer of stock graphs.**
Gap: wang2026crossalpha covers US→JP, TW, KR and HK, but not Europe. liu2026bipartite covers US→China only, exploiting non-overlapping hours. The only European GNN is paredesramos2025a3tgcn (FTSE100, survivor set). Nothing was found on AEX or Euronext.
Thesis:
- (i) A directed bipartite lead-lag graph from the US close to the next European open/close, where hours partly overlap. The question is whether the liu2026bipartite asymmetry holds with overlap.
- (ii) Zero-shot transfer of a GNN trained on an S&P 500 text graph to STOXX 600 / AEX constituents, using annual-report text to build the European graph.
- (iii) Report survivorship-safe universes.

This gives the Dutch market a home-market angle, which is useful for Frank's consulting profile.

**D8. Signed, time-activated "decoupling" edges: predicting supply-chain rewiring and whether it is priced.**
Gap: kose2026reconstructing documents 2024–25 US–China link contraction from Common Crawl but never links it to prices and does not predict rewiring. bonnet2026throwing shows redirection (winners and losers).
Thesis: on a yearly LLM-extracted firm network (semiconductors or another strategic sector), use temporal link prediction to forecast edge deletions and creations. Signed anomaly detection flags abnormal rewiring around export-control or sanction events. The pricing test asks whether firms losing cross-bloc edges underperform, and whether competitors gain. This combines the project brief's signed and temporal edges, link prediction and anomaly detection.
